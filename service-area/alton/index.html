Built on the bluffs, built to last

Alton's original town plat dates to 1817, and a lot of its housing predates central heat and air by a century or more. Christian Hill sits on the bluff above the Mississippi with homes going back to the 1830s. The Middletown Historic District covers more than 600 buildings, many of them Federal, Italianate and Queen Anne houses from the 1800s. Further from downtown the housing runs newer, from postwar ranches to recent construction.

Old houses like these can be heated and cooled well, but the work is different. Ducts have to be routed through closets and attics that were never meant for them, outdoor units have to fit on steep or narrow lots, and river-valley humidity punishes any system that's oversized or short on airflow.

Questions from Alton

Do you come out to Alton for small repairs, or only installs?

Both. We handle repairs, tune-ups, humidifiers and full system replacements in Alton. Call or book online and we'll confirm a time with you. If your heat or AC is out, tell us so we can get there as fast as possible.

Can a 19th-century house on Christian Hill have central air?

In most cases, yes. The challenge is the routing, not the equipment: running ducts through closets, attics and old chases without tearing up plaster, and finding a spot for the outdoor unit on a sloped lot. The right plan depends on the house, so we look at it in person before recommending anything.

Why does my Alton house feel damp even with the AC running?

Summer air in the river valley carries a lot of moisture, and an AC only removes it while it runs long enough. An oversized unit cools the house fast, shuts off, and leaves the humidity behind. A thermostat fan set to ON instead of AUTO can also put moisture back into the air. Checking sizing, airflow and fan settings usually points to the fix.
