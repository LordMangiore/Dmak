From St. Louis Street to the newest subdivisions

Edwardsville has been a city since 1818, and its houses show it. The St. Louis Street Historic District is lined with late-1800s Queen Anne and Italianate homes. LeClaire started in the 1890s as N.O. Nelson's planned village and became part of the city in 1934. Around them sit mid-century ranches, rentals near SIUE, and newer subdivisions on the edges of town.

Each of those asks something different of a heating and cooling system. Tall ceilings and plaster walls in the older homes make ductwork and airflow the main event. Newer houses are built tighter, so sizing, filtration and humidity control matter more than raw horsepower. We look at the house first, then the equipment, so the fix actually fits.

Questions from Edwardsville

How fast can you get to an Edwardsville home?

Edwardsville is home base for us, so in-town calls are usually the quickest ones we run. We're open daily 8am to 8pm, and most repairs are handled the same day. If your heat or AC is out, say so when you call so we can get to you as fast as possible.

Can an 1800s house near St. Louis Street heat and cool evenly?

Usually, yes. Uneven rooms in older homes almost always come down to airflow: duct runs added decades after the house was built, too few return vents, or one system trying to cover two or three floors. We start with the ductwork and returns, then recommend the smallest change that fixes it, whether that's balancing, added returns, or a properly sized system for the upper floor.

I own a rental near SIUE. What maintenance actually matters?

Three things: filters, a furnace check before winter, and an AC check before summer. Tenants rarely change filters, and a clogged one is behind a lot of no-heat and frozen-coil calls. A spring and fall tune-up catches weak parts and clogged drain lines before they turn into a weekend emergency.
